What is MUDRA? Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana

MUDRA (Micro Units Development & Refinance Agency) is a financial institution set up by the Government of India to provide funding to micro and small enterprises. Launched in 2015 under the Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana (PMMY), it aims to create an ecosystem for development and refinancing of micro enterprises.

MUDRA loans are collateral-free loans provided through banks, NBFCs, and MFIs to support non-farm income-generating activities. These include manufacturing, trading, services, and small-scale enterprises that help create jobs and boost the economy.

Key Features of MUDRA Loans

  • No Collateral Required: Loans up to ₹10 lakh don't need security
  • Quick Processing: Faster approval compared to traditional business loans
  • Flexible Usage: Can be used for working capital, equipment, or expansion
  • Refinancing Option: Existing loans can be refinanced under MUDRA
  • Government Backing: Supported by Government of India

MUDRA Loan Categories: Shishu, Kishor, Tarun

MUDRA loans are categorized into three segments based on the stage of business and loan amount required:

1. Shishu (Seed) Category

  • Loan Amount: Up to ₹50,000
  • Target Group: New entrepreneurs, startups, and micro enterprises
  • Purpose: Initial capital for starting small businesses
  • Business Stage: Seed stage or very early startup

2. Kishor (Youth) Category

  • Loan Amount: ₹50,001 to ₹5 lakh
  • Target Group: Growing micro enterprises
  • Purpose: Working capital, equipment purchase, business expansion
  • Business Stage: Established but growing businesses

3. Tarun (Adult) Category

  • Loan Amount: ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh
  • Target Group: Small enterprises with growth potential
  • Purpose: Equipment, machinery, working capital, expansion
  • Business Stage: Mature small businesses

Eligibility Criteria for MUDRA Loans

Who Can Apply?

  • Indian Citizens: Must be Indian national
  • Age: 18-65 years (some lenders may have different limits)
  • Business Type: Non-farm income-generating activities
  • Business Stage: From startup to established small enterprise
  • Income: Should have regular income from business activities

Eligible Activities

  • Manufacturing and production
  • Trading and retail
  • Services sector
  • Food processing
  • Repair and maintenance services
  • Transport services
  • Artisan and handicraft activities

Ineligible Activities

  • Agriculture and related activities
  • Real estate and property development
  • Stock market and speculative trading
  • Illegal or prohibited businesses

Interest Rates and Charges

MUDRA loan interest rates vary by lender, borrower profile, and loan category. As of March 2026:

Interest Rate Range

  • Shishu Loans: 10-14% per annum
  • Kishor Loans: 11-15% per annum
  • Tarun Loans: 12-16% per annum

Factors affecting interest rates:

  • Credit score and repayment history
  • Business vintage and financials
  • Loan amount and tenure
  • Lender's risk assessment
  • Current RBI repo rate

Other Charges

  • Processing Fee: 0.5-2% of loan amount
  • Prepayment Charges: Usually 2-3% (varies by lender)
  • Documentation Charges: ₹500-₹2,000
  • Insurance: Optional but recommended

Required Documents

Basic Documents

  • Identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN, Voter ID)
  • Address proof (utility bills, rental agreement)
  • Business registration documents (if applicable)
  • Bank statements (last 6-12 months)
  • Income proof (ITR, profit & loss statement)

Business Documents

  • Business plan or project report
  • Quotations for equipment/machinery
  • Existing loan statements (if refinancing)
  • Collateral documents (if applicable for higher amounts)

EMI Calculations and Examples

MUDRA loans typically have tenures of 1-7 years. Let's calculate EMIs for different scenarios.

Example 1: Shishu Loan - ₹50,000 at 12% for 3 years

  • Monthly interest rate = 12% ÷ 12 = 1%
  • EMI = ₹50,000 × (0.01 × (1 + 0.01)^36) ÷ ((1 + 0.01)^36 - 1) ≈ ₹1,680
  • Total interest = (₹1,680 × 36) - ₹50,000 ≈ ₹10,480

Example 2: Kishor Loan - ₹2 lakh at 13% for 5 years

  • Monthly interest rate = 13% ÷ 12 ≈ 1.083%
  • EMI ≈ ₹4,450
  • Total interest ≈ ₹67,000

Example 3: Tarun Loan - ₹7 lakh at 14% for 7 years

  • Monthly interest rate = 14% ÷ 12 ≈ 1.167%
  • EMI ≈ ₹12,800
  • Total interest ≈ ₹2.9 lakh

Application Process and Tips

How to Apply

  1. Choose a Lender: Banks, NBFCs, or MUDRA card providers
  2. Prepare Documents: Gather all required paperwork
  3. Submit Application: Online or at branch
  4. Business Assessment: Lender evaluates business viability
  5. Approval & Disbursement: Loan sanctioned and funds released

Tips for Successful Application

  • Prepare a clear business plan
  • Maintain good credit score
  • Show consistent business income
  • Apply for appropriate loan amount
  • Compare rates from multiple lenders
  • Keep documentation organized

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying for loan amount beyond business capacity
  • Poor credit history
  • Incomplete documentation
  • Not having a clear business plan
  • Ignoring interest rate comparisons

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between MUDRA and traditional business loans?

MUDRA loans are specifically for micro enterprises, offer lower amounts, and don't require collateral up to ₹10 lakh. Traditional business loans may require collateral and have different eligibility criteria.

Can I get a MUDRA loan if I have a CIBIL score below 650?

It's challenging but possible. Focus on improving your credit score first, or look for NBFCs that are more lenient. A good business plan can help compensate for a lower score.

Is MUDRA loan available for existing businesses?

Yes, MUDRA loans are available for both new and existing businesses. Existing businesses can use them for expansion, working capital, or refinancing existing loans.

What is the maximum tenure for MUDRA loans?

Typically 5-7 years, though some lenders may offer up to 10 years depending on the loan amount and business type.

Can I prepay my MUDRA loan?

Yes, most MUDRA loans allow prepayment. However, check for prepayment charges, which are usually 2-3% of the outstanding amount.

Do MUDRA loans have any subsidy or government benefits?

MUDRA loans themselves don't have direct subsidies, but they may be eligible for other government schemes. Interest rate concessions may be available for certain categories of borrowers.

Practical tips for small businesses

  • Align tenure with cash flow: Seasonal businesses may prefer slightly longer tenure to keep EMI steady.
  • Use funds productively: Prioritize inventory that turns faster or assets that boost revenue.
  • Maintain records: Clean bank statements and GST filings improve approval chances and rates.
  • Avoid overborrowing: Borrow only what you can service from operating cash flow, not optimistic projections.
  • Prepay when possible: Even small prepayments reduce interest outgo; confirm charges with your lender.

FAQs

Is MUDRA a subsidy?

MUDRA is primarily a refinance and support program; it is not a direct subsidy for all borrowers. Some schemes may have interest subvention or credit guarantees”verify specifics with your lender.

Do I need collateral?

Many MUDRA loans are collateral-free, though lenders may require guarantees or insurance as per policy.

Can I use MUDRA for trading?

Yes, trading and services are eligible activities. Ensure your documentation reflects genuine business use.
