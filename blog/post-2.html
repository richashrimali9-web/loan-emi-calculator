Home Loan vs Personal Loan: Quick Overview

In India, home loans and personal loans are two of the most popular financing options, but they serve very different purposes. A home loan is specifically for purchasing or constructing property, while a personal loan can be used for almost anything. Understanding the differences is crucial for making the right borrowing decision.

Home loans typically offer lower interest rates and longer repayment periods because the property serves as collateral, reducing the lender's risk. Personal loans, being unsecured, come with higher rates but offer more flexibility in usage.

Interest Rates: Home Loans vs Personal Loans

Interest rates are one of the biggest differentiators between these loan types. As of March 2026, home loan rates in India range from 6.5% to 9.5% per annum, depending on your credit score, lender, and loan-to-value ratio. Personal loan rates typically start from 10.5% and can go up to 24% or more.

The lower home loan rates are possible because:

  • The property acts as security for the lender
  • Government schemes like PMAY offer subsidized rates
  • Longer tenures allow lenders to earn through extended interest periods

Personal loan rates are higher because:

  • No collateral means higher risk for lenders
  • Shorter tenures mean lenders need to recover principal faster
  • Used for various purposes, some riskier than others

Eligibility Criteria

Home Loan Eligibility

  • Age: 18-70 years (co-applicant can be up to 75)
  • Income: Minimum ₹2.5-3 lakh annual income
  • Credit Score: 650+ (750+ for best rates)
  • Employment: Salaried or self-employed with stable income
  • Property Documents: Clear title, approved builder, etc.
  • Down Payment: 10-20% of property value

Personal Loan Eligibility

  • Age: 21-60 years
  • Income: Minimum ₹1.5-2 lakh annual income
  • Credit Score: 650+ (700+ for best rates)
  • Employment: Salaried with 1+ year experience, or self-employed
  • Documents: ID, address proof, income proof, bank statements
  • Collateral: Not required

Home loans generally require more documentation and verification because of the high loan amounts and property involvement. Personal loans are quicker to process but have stricter income requirements relative to loan amount.

Tax Benefits: A Major Advantage of Home Loans

One of the biggest advantages of home loans is the tax benefits available under the Income Tax Act. Personal loans offer no tax benefits.

Home Loan Tax Benefits

  • Principal Repayment: Up to ₹1.5 lakh deduction under Section 80C
  • Interest Payment: Up to ₹2 lakh deduction under Section 24(b)
  • Additional Interest: ₹50,000 extra deduction for first-time buyers under Section 80EE
  • Stamp Duty: Some states offer stamp duty concessions

These tax benefits can save you ₹50,000-₹1 lakh annually in taxes, effectively reducing your home loan's cost by 1-2%.

Personal Loan Tax Scenario

Personal loans provide no tax deductions. The interest you pay is not tax-deductible, and the EMI payments don't qualify under any tax-saving sections.

Repayment Terms and Flexibility

Home Loan Repayment

  • Tenure: 5-30 years (typically 15-20 years)
  • EMI Structure: Fixed or floating rate
  • Prepayment: Allowed, often with penalties for fixed-rate loans
  • Part-payment: Usually allowed once or twice a year
  • Foreclosure: Possible after 5-7 years, with charges

Personal Loan Repayment

  • Tenure: 1-7 years (typically 3-5 years)
  • EMI Structure: Fixed rate only
  • Prepayment: Allowed, usually without penalties
  • Part-payment: Allowed, often with minimal fees
  • Foreclosure: Possible, with prepayment charges

Home loans offer longer tenures, which means lower monthly EMIs but higher total interest. Personal loans have shorter tenures, resulting in higher EMIs but lower total interest.

When to Choose a Home Loan vs Personal Loan

Choose a Home Loan When:

  • You want to buy, construct, or renovate a property
  • You need a large loan amount (₹10 lakh+)
  • You want tax benefits on interest and principal
  • You can afford long-term commitment (15-20 years)
  • You have property documents ready
  • You want lower interest rates

Choose a Personal Loan When:

  • You need funds for education, medical expenses, or debt consolidation
  • You want quick approval and disbursement (within days)
  • You don't have collateral or property documents
  • You prefer shorter repayment periods
  • You need flexibility in loan usage
  • You have good credit score for better rates

Real-World Examples and Calculations

Example 1: Property Purchase vs Home Renovation

Rahul wants to buy a ₹50 lakh apartment. He has ₹10 lakh savings for down payment.

  • Home Loan Option: ₹40 lakh at 8% for 20 years
  • EMI: ₹33,400 per month
  • Total Interest: ₹60 lakh over 20 years
  • Tax Benefit: ₹2 lakh/year on interest + ₹1.5 lakh on principal

If Rahul uses a personal loan for the same amount:

  • Personal Loan Option: ₹40 lakh at 14% for 5 years
  • EMI: ₹95,500 per month
  • Total Interest: ₹17.3 lakh over 5 years
  • Tax Benefit: None

The home loan EMI is much more affordable, and tax benefits make it even more attractive.

Example 2: Education Expenses

Priya needs ₹5 lakh for her child's education abroad.

  • Personal Loan Option: ₹5 lakh at 13% for 4 years
  • EMI: ₹14,200 per month
  • Total Interest: ₹1.8 lakh over 4 years

A home loan wouldn't be suitable here since it's not for property purchase.

Example 3: Debt Consolidation

Amit has multiple credit card debts totaling ₹3 lakh at 36% interest.

  • Personal Loan Option: ₹3 lakh at 15% for 3 years
  • EMI: ₹10,800 per month
  • Total Interest: ₹88,800 over 3 years
  • Savings: ₹1.5 lakh compared to credit card interest

This reduces his interest burden significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a personal loan to buy property?

Technically yes, but it's not recommended. Personal loans have higher interest rates and no tax benefits. Lenders may also restrict usage for property purchases. Home loans are specifically designed for property financing.

Which loan is easier to get approved?

Personal loans are generally easier and faster to approve since they require less documentation and no collateral. Home loans involve property valuation and legal checks, making the process longer.

Can I convert a personal loan to a home loan?

Some lenders allow balance transfer from personal loan to home loan if you purchase property. However, this involves additional processing and may not always be beneficial due to different interest rates and terms.

Do both loans require credit score checks?

Yes, both require good credit scores (650+). However, home loans may be more lenient for first-time buyers with lower scores, while personal loans strictly enforce credit score requirements.

Which loan affects my credit score more?

Both affect your credit score similarly. Timely payments improve your score, while delays hurt it. Home loans involve larger amounts, so managing them well can boost your score faster.

Can self-employed individuals get both loans?

Yes, but home loans for self-employed require 2-3 years of ITR, while personal loans are more flexible. Self-employed individuals often get better rates on home loans due to tax benefits.

Tax treatment (India)

  • Home loans: Interest deduction under Section 24(b) up to prescribed limits, and principal under Section 80C (subject to conditions). Additional benefits may apply for affordable housing categories.
  • Personal loans: Generally no tax benefit. If used for business or certain assets, interest may be deductible subject to documentation and tax rules. Consult a tax advisor.

How to choose between a home loan and personal loan

Choose a home loan if:

  • You are purchasing or constructing property and can provide collateral.
  • You want the lowest possible rate and longer tenure for affordability.
  • You want potential tax benefits on interest/principal (subject to rules).

Choose a personal loan if:

  • You need funds quickly for short-term needs and cannot/should not offer collateral.
  • You can comfortably repay in 1–5 years despite higher interest.
  • Your loan size is small-to-medium and documentation time must be minimal.

Practical decision checklist

  • Purpose: asset (home) vs general consumption/short-term need.
  • Eligibility: income stability, credit score (ideally 750+), existing obligations.
  • Rate vs speed: lower rate with more steps (home) vs faster but costlier (personal).
  • Tenure comfort: longer with lower EMI vs shorter with higher EMI.
  • Total cost: include fees, insurance, and potential prepayment charges.
  • Tax: home loan benefits may tilt the decision for eligible borrowers.

Actionable tips

  • Compare at least 3 offers: Use our calculator to model EMI and total interest for each quote.
  • Negotiate: With a strong credit profile and stable income, ask lenders to match your best rate.
  • Avoid very long tenures by default: They reduce EMI but inflate total interest; plan prepayments when cash flow allows.
  • Mind the fine print: Prepayment/foreclosure rules, part-payment minimums, and processing fee refunds matter.
  • Consider balance transfer: If your existing rate is materially higher than market rates, explore refinancing after factoring fees.

Which saves more interest: low rate with long tenure, or higher rate with short tenure?

Usually, a lower rate with the shortest affordable tenure saves the most interest. Use the calculator to balance EMI comfort and total cost.

Will a personal loan hurt my home loan eligibility later?

It can. Lenders consider existing EMIs when calculating your FOIR/DTI. A personal loan EMI reduces your eligible home loan amount.

Are prepayment charges applicable?

Many floating home loans to individuals have zero prepayment penalty. Personal loans often have prepayment/foreclosure charges. Check your agreement.
