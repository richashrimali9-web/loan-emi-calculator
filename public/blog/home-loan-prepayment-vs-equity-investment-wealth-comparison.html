Home Loan Prepayment vs. Equity Investment: The 20-Year Wealth Comparison

Updated: April 2026 | Analysis for Salaried Professionals

Every Indian salaried professional with a home loan faces the same dilemma at the end of the year: "Should I use my bonus to prepay my home loan at 8.5% interest, or should I invest it in a Nifty 50 Index Fund expecting 12% returns?"

On the surface, the math seems simple: 12% is greater than 8.5%. But once you factor in tax benefits, psychological comfort, and the power of compounding, the "best" answer becomes much more nuanced.

The Math: Return on Investment (ROI) vs. Cost of Debt

To compare these two accurately, we must look at the Effective Cost and Net Returns.

Factor Prepayment (Saving Interest) Equity Investment (Gaining Wealth)
Nominal Rate 8.50% (Loan Interest) 12.00% (Market Return)
Tax Implications Loses Sec 24(b) benefit on interest Pay 12.5% Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG)
Effective "Profit" ~7.50% (Guaranteed) ~10.50% (Variable)

A 20-Year Scenario Comparison

Let's assume you have a ₹50,000 annual surplus. You have 20 years left on a ₹40 Lakh home loan at 8.5%.

Scenario A: Prepayment

If you prepay ₹50,000 every year, you will reduce your loan tenure from 20 years to roughly 16 years and 4 months.
Total Interest Saved: ₹14.8 Lakhs.

Scenario B: Investing in Equity SIP

If you invest that ₹50,000 annually in an equity mutual fund (SIP) for 20 years at a 12% CAGR:
Final Corpus: ₹40.3 Lakhs.
Tax-adjusted (LTCG): ~₹36.5 Lakhs.

The Psychological Factor: Debt-Free vs. Wealthy

The numbers suggest that Investing wins by a margin of nearly ₹20 Lakhs over 20 years. However, this assumes you have the discipline to stay invested through market crashes (like 2008 or 2020) and that your income stays stable.

For many Indian families, the psychological peace of mind that comes from owning a "Debt-Free Home" is worth more than the mathematical surplus. Being debt-free reduces your "Monthly Burn Rate," providing a massive safety net in case of job loss.

The "Hybrid Strategy" for 2026

Most experts recommend a hybrid approach. Instead of choosing one, do both:

  1. Emergency First: Ensure you have 6 months of EMIs in a liquid fund.
  2. Prepay 1 Extra EMI: Prepaying just one extra EMI per year can reduce a 20-year loan to 17 years.
  3. SIP the Rest: Put any remaining surplus into equity. This way, you reduce your debt while simultaneously building a wealth corpus.

Final Verdict

If your home loan interest rate is above 9.5%, prioritize prepayment. If it is below 8.5% and you have a 10-year horizon, equity investments will likely build significantly more wealth.
